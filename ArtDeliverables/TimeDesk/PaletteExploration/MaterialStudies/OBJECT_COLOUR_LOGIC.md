# Object colour decisions

Use this order for every object: material, plausible finish, balance in the complete room. A palette is a source of compatible accents and undertones, not a replacement for the material's identity.

| Object | Material logic | Permitted colour direction |
|---|---|---|
| Desk | Real walnut, oak or elm; grain remains visible | Natural light-to-dark browns, red-brown or smoked finishes |
| Office pad | Leather, linoleum or felt; construction and roughness remain readable | Burgundy, olive, navy, tobacco or muted grey; choose against the desk |
| CRT / phone | Moulded ABS, enamel or bakelite | Aged ivory, grey, black or a restrained manufactured colour; avoid making all equipment match |
| Lamp | Enamel or glass shade, metal stem | Bottle green, ivory or oxblood enamel; metal retains a distinct finish |
| Glass | Transparent glazing with reflected light and view beyond | Very light neutral/cool cast; reflections and transmission, not opaque paint |
| Floor | Existing stone/tile, subdued matte/satin finish | Warm grey/taupe with natural variation, separate from glass and crowds |
| Ceiling | Plaster or concrete, only where already visible | Cooler light neutral; never invent a new ceiling |
| Metal | Aluminium, steel, brass or bronze | Believable finish with neutral highlights; coloured paint only on plausible painted parts |
| Teleporter | Coherent machine with one energy system | Stable gunmetal/nickel rim, single muted teal energy colour; no palette wheel |
| Background buildings | Different brick, stone, painted masonry and concrete buildings | Several plausible local colours together; distance softens them, not a universal hue filter |
| Crowds | Anonymous layered silhouettes for atmosphere | Low-contrast grey-blue, grey-brown and graphite masses; remain distinct from the floor |

Assess balance at the whole-scene scale: warm wood beside cool stone/glass, light papers against darker work surfaces, restrained coloured equipment, varied quieter city behind. A repeated accent can connect a few objects; it must not spread across unrelated surfaces. Shared light unifies the scene without erasing local material colours.

The ten studies vary plausible finish combinations only. They are proposals; no Unity materials, lighting, scene geometry or original floor have been changed. Character generation remains paused until this task is resolved.
