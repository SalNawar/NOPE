# Material-led colour direction — 2026-09-25

Supersedes both prior palette boards. Saleh rejected the monochrome grading, then arbitrary multicolour material assignments. The revised goal is believable materials with subtle colour direction.

GOAL: a believable soft-anime game environment, subtly art-directed. Palette is a STYLE GUIDE, NOT a command to recolour every material.
Reference: use the original game's office screenshot as composition/layout anchor, NOT its flat rendering. Same central circular time portal, the bank of tall windows and city beyond, mixed anonymous crowd silhouettes in hall, boards at both sides, foreground wooden desk, left CRT/keyboard, middle rotary phone and till, right banker's lamp and stationery. No changed furniture shapes or new architecture. No painted colour-block art.
MATERIALS MUST READ:
1. Glass wall/window panes are TRANSPARENT glazing, not opaque colour panels. See the city through them, overlaid by subtle broad pale reflections of the room, a few soft diagonal highlights, double-surface edge reflections and thin metal mullions. Fresnel reflections stronger at oblique edges, high transmission in central panes. Keep glass clean, lightly cool-neutral, never saturated cyan.
2. Individual BACKGROUND BUILDINGS have DIFFERENT believable materials and colours simultaneously: weathered terracotta brick, pale warm limestone, blue-grey concrete, muted sage-painted masonry, tan/ochre stone, occasional dark red brick. NOT repeated identical colours. Mixed warm lit and cool dark windows. Muted by atmospheric distance and viewed THROUGH glass. Same natural varied city across all options; NEVER tint the whole skyline to the option's accent.
3. The desk is real natural WOOD with restrained recognisable grain, broad soft sheen and a clean surface. It never becomes a single flat painted palette colour.
4. CRT casing is aged ABS PLASTIC: rounded moulded corners, seams, matte surface; display has dark reflective GLASS with restrained room reflections and a small Windows95/98 UI. Its screen is not a flat opaque blue rectangle.
5. TELEPORTER IS ONE COHERENT MACHINE. Use the SAME brushed gunmetal/nickel ring in every study, subtle screws and continuous muted teal inner energy aperture, thin single-colour teal inner light. No multicoloured segments, rainbow rim, gold/copper patchwork, or palette-dependent portal energy colours. Its glow is low, local, not a room grade.
6. Office pad is its own MATERIAL and specified colour in each study. It is leather or wool felt with realistic edge construction, restrained shading. Do not leave the same anonymous grey pad in every version. Never a blue cutting grid.
7. Metal reads metallic through small sharp neutral highlights; plastics/enamel have broad restrained reflections; wood grain and leather/felt remain clean, simple, stylised, no gritty photoreal noise.
ART DIRECTION: soft clean 2D anime-inspired rendering with rounded objects, restrained cel shadows, subtle material highlights. Realistic material separation, not flat vector colour blocks. Retrofuturist Art Deco CIVIC BUREAUCRACY meets Windows95/98 computers. Deco is geometry and restrained hardware, not gold coating on everything. Neutral white daylight and gentle warm local lamp light; no global colour wash. Preserve clear midtones. Mix warm natural wood/paper with cool stone/glass and carefully limited coloured objects. The chosen accent family occupies about10–20% of the view. Never recolour floors, every column, crowds or buildings to match a named palette. Keep neutral stone original floor, paper ivory, crowd anonymous subdued cool-grey masses.

REQUIRED COLOUR LOGIC AND VISUAL SEPARATION:
- Original stone FLOOR stays warm grey/taupe with subtle tile variation. Not blue glass, not same hue as people.
- CEILING, only if already visible in this camera, is cooler pale plaster/concrete with soft ambient shading. Do not invent a new ceiling to fill the view.
- GLASS is mostly colourless TRANSMISSION of a varied city, plus understated pale cool reflections. It must never read as solid grey building material.
- Each major BACKGROUND BUILDING has an independent identity: at left one terracotta brick facade; centre pale limestone next to blue-grey concrete; right a subdued sage-painted block and buff ochre brick. Different hue families visibly coexist. Distant buildings desaturate into atmospheric blue-grey; do not make every nearby building grey/beige. Windows have dark cool panes, neutral reflections and occasional warm lights.
- CROWDS remain anonymous overlapping simple masses, no faces or identifying clothing detail, using 2–3 subdued grey-blue, grey-brown and graphite tones with depth. Clearly darker than the pale floor/glass, lower contrast than desk props. Not white cardboard cutouts and never cloned building colours.
- DESK remains natural warm wood. PAD reads as the specified independent leather/felt material, with its own quiet hue, edge and finish.
The glass, stone, plaster, wood, cloth, skinless crowd masses and distant city must never be visually treated as one flat colour field. Shared white daylight unifies them WITHOUT erasing their own local colours. No global sepia/teal wash. Material rendering remains clean stylised soft-anime, not photorealistic varnish or dramatic cinematic lighting. Keep banners blank of invented logos and slogan text; reference boards keep only their existing basic readouts.

## Ten finish studies

These are concept previews, not in-game captures or replacements. The choices below are art direction, not RGB conversion rules. Keep the neutral base, material behaviour, city diversity and portal identity stable. Adjust a selected scheme only after viewing it in the actual game.

### 01 Archive Brass

Wood: smoked walnut with visible brown wood grain. Pad: deep oxblood leather, softened burgundy-brown, fine grain and a stitched edge. CRT: aged ivory ABS plastic. Phone: near-black bakelite with a soft reflected highlight. Lamp: bottle-green enamel with a brass stem. Hardware: restrained aged brass. UI: classic muted navy-blue Windows95 title bar. Accent reference: #6B3D3C, #334F43, #AD9058.
