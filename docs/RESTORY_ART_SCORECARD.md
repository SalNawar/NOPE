# ReStory art-direction criteria and current self-assessment

Reference: `ArtDeliverables/TimeDesk/DeskFinish/ReStory_current_reference.png`.
Assessed result: actual Unity Game View `ArtDeliverables/TimeDesk/DeskFinish/Iterations/36_iteration_3_final.png`, after the three authorized passes.

These are subjective visual judgments, not measured production-quality metrics. Reference quality is 10/10 for each category. Scores reflect the delivered image, not effort or asset count. The different civic/science-fiction setting, absent characters, blank CRT and game-controlled text are intentional and are not deductions.

| Criterion | What the reference demonstrates | Current score | Visible shortfall |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Cohesive stylization | Props, architecture, paintings and small graphics share a consistent degree of simplification and edge treatment. | 4/10 | The computer, scan-derived statues, simplified paintings and procedural city still look like separate asset families. |
| Prop shape and construction | Characterful silhouettes, believable thickness, controlled bevels, deliberate proportions and convincing assemblies. | 6/10 | PC and till construction is substantially better; simpler desk props and hall machinery still have a generic modeled appearance. |
| Painted surfaces and wear | Broad designed colour variation, quiet texture detail, and wear concentrated where the object is handled or exposed. | 4/10 | Reused corner chips and surface patches feel applied across objects; larger surfaces lack the reference's authored variation. |
| Lighting and depth | Clear key/fill relationship, soft contact, warm/cool separation and a controlled range of light and shadow. | 4/10 | Forms are readable, but hall lighting is broad and rigid; the exterior is dark and the whole image lacks the reference's luminous depth. |
| Palette and material separation | Rich but organized greens, warm timber, neutral hardware, blue work surface and small colour accents. | 5/10 | The intended families are now present, but many surfaces remain dull, with less nuanced transitions between materials. |
| Focal hierarchy and framing | Eye movement leads from foreground tools and the work surface to the customer opening; peripheral objects support that focus. | 4/10 | PC placement helps, but the portal, broad empty floor and side boards compete; the hall occupies a different and weaker visual hierarchy. |
| Purposeful density and grouping | Dense functional clusters, varied heights and overlaps, with usable negative space where the player works. | 3/10 | Desk items remain separated and evenly spaced; the hall reads sparse. Density must come from related useful objects, not random filler. |
| Environmental storytelling | Repaired, used and personal objects make the place feel occupied, with each detail supporting its function and history. | 3/10 | Famous exhibits provide meaning, but feel displayed rather than embedded in the daily life of an underfunded public office. |
| Background design and distance | Distinct architectural forms, layered values and varied detail scales create a convincing place beyond the work area. | 4/10 | Facade variants and modeled windows are clearer, but megablocks remain repetitive and insufficiently separated in depth. |
| Consistency of final finish | Comparable care across foreground, midground and background; intentional edges, readable small details and no distracting defects. | 5/10 | Foreground hardware is ahead of the hall and city. The scene's quality is uneven, even though the targeted runtime checks pass. |

**Total: 42/100.** Equal category weights. This is not ReStory-quality art yet.

Acceptance bar: at least 8/10 in every category, with no category hidden by a high overall average. Evaluate from the fixed game camera at normal viewing size; require readable forms, intentional groups, coherent surfaces and matching finish across the image. Extra polygons, more props, higher resolution or a passing console check do not by themselves increase the art score.

Highest-impact remaining work: a coherent style across all asset families; more authored, functional groupings; and stronger background/light depth. Composition changes remain deferred by the user's instruction. This scorecard is an assessment, not authorization to begin a fourth iteration.
