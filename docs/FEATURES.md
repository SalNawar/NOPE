# NOPE — Feature Inventory

The contract of what the game does. **Any change that removes or alters a
listed behaviour must update this file in the same commit.** Tested = covered
by the EditMode suite in `Assets/Tests/EditMode`.

- [ ] Endings (`EndingService`): fired (firing threshold on stability) and bankrupt (bankruptcy threshold) are checked after every verdict, at the end of a shift that applied dialog consequences, and at sleep (the debug Skip Day sleeps too); Retirement (day 15) and the attribute epilogues only at sleep, so the run ends after day 15 is played, and an epilogue whose attribute total is reached (Art 41, Science 26, Democracy 9: the 65th percentile of perfect play's day-15 totals over 50 simulated runs; ties go to Democracy, Science, Art) replaces Retirement (selection tested: `EndingRulesTests`; the checks' timing is checked in Unity)

## World

- [ ] Real countries as lineages: Egypt, Iraq, Greece, Italy, China, Japan, Britain, Germany × Ancient / Medieval / Early modern / Industrial / Modern = 40 places, plus eight Future places (the office's own time, 2150), of which only the leading nation's is in the world (see History)
- [ ] Each place has a moment and year, five authored facts (currency, language, technology, capital, ruler), a Culture (dress) fact derived from its wardrobe's signature items ("top hat / poke bonnet", men's / women's, or one item for both), a wardrobe per gender (outfit, hair, facial hair, headwear, accessory; one signature item each) and 8 male + 8 female period names (the Culture value and the label rule tested: `LooksTests`)
- [ ] Travellers are born 18–70 years before their place's year (set by the generator); ancient dates print as BCE (dates tested: `BirthDatesTests`)
- [ ] Visitor roles: Artist, Diplomat, Merchant, Scientist, Soldier, Wanderer (their accepted sends move Democracy / Science / Art); roles never change a traveller's dress; names come only from the claimed place's period names (a liar's name is part of their cover)
- [ ] Every traveller asks to go home to the place they claim, and their name, birth date, papers and Citizen Record all come from that claim. An honest traveller really comes from there: every field agrees with the books and the record. A liar really comes from another of today's places (any country, any of today's eras) and travels under the claimed place's cover identity, but the disguise leaks tells carrying the true home's value, on the papers (a Currency, Language or Technology tell rewrites every field of that category; a birth-date tell keeps the record's day and month with a year from the true home's birth years), in speech (their answer to one of today's day-gated questions gives the true home's value while the papers show the cover) or in dress (one garment, the true home's signature item for their gender, worn over the claimed look). A category leaks on one channel only. The day plan's tell channels set where tells may appear (day 1 papers only; day 2 papers and answers; from day 3 papers, answers and dress); a spoken tell needs its question askable that day, gated by day alone, and a book (or the Citizen Record for a birth date). Each liar leaks the day's tell count (`DayPlanSO` "tell count", 1 on days 1–6), capped by the categories that can carry a tell for them. The liar chance is the blueprint's contradiction chance plus the slot-machine modifier and forgery-risk effects; rule violators and honest premades never lie (a premade authored as a liar always lies, from their own true place, through papers or answers, never dress); a rolled liar with no possible tell stays honest and a warning is logged (tell selection, channels, eligibility and the may-lie exemptions tested: `LiesTests`, `ForgeryTests`, `InterviewDayTests`, `LooksTests`; answers tested: `InterviewTests`; the liar chance, cover records and whole-day behaviour are checked in Unity, not by the EditMode suite)
- [ ] Traveller gender is recorded from the claimed place's name lists ("Marcus II" counts as Marcus; premades carry an authored gender; "Subject #n" is unknown, and their look draws a gender); the desk's opener uses it for the honorific (sir/madam/traveller) (tested: `TravellerGendersTests`, `NameRosterTests`, `InterviewTests`)
- [ ] Premade characters: ten named, long-dead, non-ruler people who lived in their claimed place and era (five women, five men), drawn whole; none claims or comes from the Future (its place is in the world only while its nation leads); Senenmut stands in day 1 slot 3 and "Socrates" (an impostor from Republican Rome) in day 2 slot 6; every other slot of days 2–6 has a 5% chance of one from the day's pool; once per run when presented (a met forced premade leaves an ordinary traveller in the slot); never on a rule violator's slot, and violators are never placed in a forced premade's slot; honest unless authored as liars; their own opener and Citizen Records note; scoring as legendaries (bonus pay, extra stability risk) (scheduling tested: `PremadesTests`, `ViolatorSlotsTests`; the opener tested: `InterviewTests`; the met flag's format tested: `GatesTests`; whole-day scheduling checked in Unity)

## Translation

- [ ] Every place has a tongue (`world_source.json` `places[].tongue`); a traveller's papers and speech use the claimed place's tongue, never their true home's, so the tongue never shows or hides a lie. English (Britain from the Early modern era, the Future included) is always readable. From day 2 (`translation.fromDay`, announced in that morning's paper by a one-shot trigger) a foreign tongue is drawn in its script: the values of place facts on the scanned papers (Coin of Issue, Native Tongue, Declared Device, Bond Currency; never field labels, titles, the Full Name or the Date of Birth) and every traveller line, in the bubble and the transcript; digits, spaces and punctuation stay as they are, and the same value always looks the same, on papers and in speech (the day's rules and what uses the tongue tested: `TranslationTests`; the glyphs tested: `PseudoscriptTests`)

- [ ] Correct decision: pay (base × pay-rate multiplier + timeline bonuses; legendary bonus)
- [ ] Wrong decision: citation (free warnings, then escalating penalties), stability loss
- [ ] Evidence-gated denial ("Log a deviation from the papers, the traveller's answers or their dress before denying"): denying a liar with **zero** documented discrepancies counts as a wrong decision (citation and stability loss; a deduction once the free warnings are used) even though the visitor lied (`requireEvidenceToDeny` toggle) (the unproven-denial rule tested: `VerdictRulesTests`; the citation and deduction are Assembly-CSharp (`ShiftScoring`) and checked in Unity, not by the EditMode suite)
- [ ] Directive-violation denials never need scanned evidence (the gate exemption tested: `VerdictRulesTests`)
- [ ] Only provable tells are generated (tested: `ForgeryTests`, `LiesTests`, `BirthDatesTests`, `InterviewDayTests`, `LooksTests`): a place fact is a tell only when a reference book covers it and the true home's value differs from the claim's and belongs to no other of today's places (so the books prove it and the origin proof names the home); it shows on the papers only when a paper prints it, in speech only when its question is askable that day and gated by day alone, in dress only when the Costume Guide exists and the true home's signature item for the traveller's gender is a real, leakable item (never the whole outfit), not hidden by the disguise, not drawn from the same art as the disguise's own item in that slot, not labelled like it and not on the confusable list; a birth-date tell keeps day and month and takes a year from the true home's birth years, never the record's (provable via citizen records); names are never tells
- [ ] Every active travel rule gets at least one violator in the first half of the queue (`DayPlanSO` "guarantee rule violators", on by default) (slots tested: `ViolatorSlotsTests`)
- [ ] Timeline impacts apply only on ACCEPT and land on the claimed place (where the traveller is sent), liar or not; sends tracked per era; an accepted liar also carries their true home's Technology there (see History)
- [ ] A new run ranks every place's attributes (Future places have none) from the baselines silently; each night's news then reports a newly DOMINANT attribute only for places on tomorrow's plan ("Science is now DOMINANT in …"; no "rising" line) (tiers tested: `DominanceTiersTests`, `ScoreRankingTests`; the tomorrow filter is checked in Unity)
