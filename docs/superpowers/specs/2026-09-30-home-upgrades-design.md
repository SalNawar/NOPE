# Home upgrades: the House tree (design)

*2026-09-30 · drafted by Claude from Saleh's direction of 2026-09-29 and his design rules of the same day · read-only map of `main` `e30184c` and of the Orders track's branch `feat/orders` at `06e4883` (not on `main` yet) · the code wins over this text · it extends the portals spec v3 (`origin/design/portals`, `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-30-portals-design.md`, **S**): its HM1-HM3 and §7 (four house upgrades) are the starting point, and this spec replaces §7's list and HM2 with a larger tree.*

Saleh, verbatim (2026-09-29, relayed by the orchestrator):

> "way more than four and go into upgrading not just sickness: there is food, better housing, security; add more money sinks"

The work upgrades (scanners, translators, protocols, contacts, portal repairs) move to the PC's Orders app (the Orders track, S OR1). **Home keeps the expenses, family care, the slot machine and sleep, and gains the House tree.**

Saleh's design rules (2026-09-29) apply; rule 4 matters most here: every price, effect and prerequisite is data he edits in the engine (the content spreadsheet for the tree, the Inspector for the household knobs).

## 1. Decisions

| Id | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| HU1 | **Five categories**, each a band of the tree: Food, Housing, Security, Health, Comfort (appended to the Orders track's `UpgradeBranch` after its four, so one enum and one tree model serve both venues). | Saleh named food, housing and security; the brief adds health (the cabinet and filters) and pure money sinks with small comforts. One tree model, not a parallel one (merge criteria, intent audit). |
| HU2 | **18 house upgrades** (§2), 3-4 per category, each with a one-off price, an optional nightly upkeep, one to three effects on the family's knobs, and prerequisites inside Home's tree only (a prerequisite sold at Orders is refused, S's `UpgradeTree.Problems` rule kept; its "a Home upgrade requires nothing" rule is dropped, Saleh's new direction). | "way more than four"; "add more money sinks": upkeep keeps an upgrade costing after it is bought. |
| HU3 | **Data in the content spreadsheet**: `world_source.json` `home.upgrades[]` (one row per upgrade: `id`, `name`, `category`, `cost`, `upkeep`, `requires`, the effect columns, `blurb`), mapped in `ContentSheetMap` (a `home` one-row sheet, a `homeUpgrades` row sheet), generated by Generate World into `Assets/Data/World/Home/Upgrade_House_<Id>.asset` and `Effect_House_<Id>.asset` (one op per non-zero effect column, permanent: duration -1), listed in the library after the hand-authored upgrades and effects. | Saleh's rule 4 through the sheet, as the translators already are (`translation.packs[]`); S HM2 had four hand-authored assets, but 18 rows with prerequisites are edited far more easily as one sheet. The household base knobs stay Inspector knobs on `GameConfig_Default` (§4). |
| HU4 | **Effects through the effect system**: each house upgrade's unlock effect carries household ops (§3), summed by `TimelineEffects.SumFloat` and read by `HomeEconomy` through one Domain rule, `HomeRules.Adjusted(value, bonus)` (never below 0; a cost `HomeRules.Cost`, rounded half to even). | S HM3: the effect system already carries Diplomatic Contacts' pay boost; no second modifier path. |
| HU5 | **Bought at Home, owned at once, in force from the next night** (tonight's bills, drift and break-in were settled when Home opened). The Buy button needs every prerequisite owned and the wallet to cover the price (`Orders.StateOf` with an empty log: Locked, Orderable = Buyable, TooDear, Owned; no second state rule). | S HM2's timing; the state rule is the Orders track's. |
| HU6 | **A night event, the break-in** (Security's reason): from `breakInFromDay` (2), each night a roll on its own stream `Seeds.ForBreakIns(daySeed)` (salt "BRKN", with a distinctness test) below `breakInChance` (0.08, + `BreakInChance` ops) means someone got in while the clerk was at work: they take `breakInShare` (0.25, + `BreakInShare` ops) of a positive wallet, at most `breakInMaxLoss` (60 cr). It is rolled first at Home, before the bills, shown as the bill's "Break-in" line and counted in the night's household costs on the statement. | The game has no theft or eviction event; security needs something to protect from. Seeded, so a reload or Continue replays it. |
| HU7 | **Mood and recovery** (Food's and Comfort's small new effect): the household's mood is the sum of the `Mood` ops owned; each night a sick member (condition above 0) who did not worsen gets one point better on a roll on `Seeds.ForRecovery(daySeed)` (salt "RCVR", mixed with the member's place, as the family's stream is; a distinctness test) below `HomeRules.RecoveryChance(mood) = min(maxRecoveryChance, mood x recoveryPerMood)` (0.04 a point, at most 0.5). The expenses step shows the mood and tonight's changes ("Kid got worse. Partner is feeling better."). | "better mood" needs a consequence the player can see; recovery is the humane counterpart of the existing drift, and reads through the same family rows. |
| HU8 | **The House panel** (§6): the old `ShopPanel` object and art, re-laid out by the builder on every build (size, the tree area, the detail strip; the old `ShopRows` container removed), drawing five category columns of cards (icon slot, name, price or state), each column the category's band of `UpgradeTree.Layout` turned on its side (a slot is a sub-column, a tier a row, so a chain runs straight down), connectors from a prerequisite to its dependants, and a detail strip for the selected card (the blurb, the effects in words, the upkeep, "Needs: …", Buy). Text at 22 px or more on the 1080p reference (15 px or more at 720p), on opaque plates. | "a visual panel of the tree … readable at 720p"; reusing the panel keeps the art and its hand-wired sprite. Bands as columns fit the tree's shape (3-4 items a category, chains, Health two of them) in four rows and six sub-columns, where the Orders tree's left-to-right bands would need seven rows. |
| HU9 | **The House panel uses the Orders track's `UpgradeTree.Layout`**, given the venue to draw (`Layout(nodes, UpgradeVenue.Home)`); `UpgradeTree.BranchesOf(venue)` names each venue's bands, and `Problems` refuses a branch of the other venue. Comfort is a chain (Plant, Photograph, Radio, Bed) so no category is wider than two slots. Tested in `UpgradeTreeTests`. | One tree layout and one node model (`TreeNode`) for both venues; the minimal extension of the owning system (the build first planned a second, outline layout: the coordinator asked for the one layout). |
| HU10 | **The balance simulation buys** (§9): a Domain `HousePolicy` picks, each night after the bills, the cheapest buyable house upgrade while the wallet stays above a reserve (`BalanceSimulation.HouseReserve`, 60 cr: one night's household), and treats a member whose condition reached 3 when the wallet covers it; the summary reports what was bought and when, the upkeep paid, the break-ins and the care, before and after. | The money figures must include the new sinks (the brief); a plain "cheapest affordable" buyer would bankrupt itself on upkeep. |
| HU11 | **Merging Orders**: Home lists `venue = Home` upgrades only; the eight office upgrades leave Home (they are Orders nodes). `UpgradeBranch` keeps the Orders track's numbers (Desk 0 … Contacts 3) and Home's five follow (Food 4 … Comfort 8), pinned in `SerializedEnumsTests`. | Resolve toward "Home = expenses, care, slots, sleep, house upgrades". |

## 2. The tree (the first cut; every number is a spreadsheet cell)

Prices are set against phase 23's economy: perfect play at the shift clock's pace nets about 15 cr a day after the household's 60 cr, so a careful clerk affords the cheap comforts early and one or two big items a run; upkeep makes every food plan and subscription a nightly choice. `requires` lists ids inside the same tree.

With everything owned: the worsen chance 0.25 falls to 0 (never below it), rent 30 rises to 47, upkeep is 20 cr a night, care costs 3, the medical drain 0 a point, the mood 14 (recovery 0.5, the cap), break-ins 0.01 a night for at most 10 % of the wallet. Owning all costs 1,980 cr: a run's worth of perfect play cannot buy the whole tree, which is the point of a money sink.

## 3. Effect ops (appended to `EffectOpType`, pinned from now on)

All continuous (summed while active, `EffectOps.ActsWhileActive` true, so a dialog's effect may not carry them), in this order after `SetFact` (17): `HouseholdExpense` (cr a night on rent and utilities), `SicknessChance` (+chance a member worsens a night), `CareCost` (cr on a treatment), `Upkeep` (cr a night, its own bill line), `MedicalDrain` (cr on each condition point), `Mood` (points), `BreakInChance` (+chance), `BreakInShare` (+share of the wallet taken). If the Orders or portals track appends first, ours are renumbered after theirs.

## 4. The household knobs (Inspector, `GameConfig_Default`; Generate World never writes it)

Kept: `baseDailyExpense` 30, `expensePerFamilyMember` 15, `expensePerConditionPoint` 1, `conditionCareCost` 8, `conditionWorsenChance` 0.25, `maxFamilyCondition` 10, `slotSpinCost` 10. New: `recoveryPerMood` 0.04, `maxRecoveryChance` 0.5, `breakInFromDay` 2, `breakInChance` 0.08, `breakInShare` 0.25, `breakInMaxLoss` 60.

## 5. The evening

1. **Expenses** (Home opens, `DayCycle.OpenHome(world, lib, config, daySeed)`, in this order so a reload replays it): the break-in roll, the bill (rent and utilities, family, medical drain, upkeep, the break-in's loss), the night's drift and recovery. The panel lists the lines, the mood, tonight's changes and the family rows with Treat (the care cost with the house effects).
2. **House** (the tree, §6). "Bought tonight: in force from tomorrow night."
3. **Slot** (unchanged).
4. **Sleep**: with the Radio owned, the panel adds one radio line (`home.radio[]`, the day's line by `day % count`, no random draw).

The statement (TC-960): household costs = the bill including the break-in, plus care; purchases = house upgrades and spins (unchanged columns).

- Connectors: 3 px lines from a card's left edge down to each dependant's middle (one `Graphic` drawing every segment).

- Every text 20 reference px or more (card names 26, their states 24, shrinking to fit no lower than 20: 13 px at 720p), dark ink on the cream plates, contrast checked by `UiContrastCheck` at build and measured on screen at 720p.

## 7. Saves

Nothing new: a house upgrade is an owned upgrade (`unlockedUpgradeIds`) and its effect an active effect entry; mood, break-ins and recovery are recomputed from them and the day seed. The save version stays.

## 8. Tests (EditMode, Domain first)

`HomeRulesTests` (Adjusted, Cost rounding and floor, RecoveryChance cap, Recovers pinned to its stream, Night's order, BreakIn pinned, BreakInLoss: positive wallet only, share, cap); `SeedsTests` (the two salts distinct and pinned; each stream apart from every other); `SerializedEnumsTests` (`EffectOpType` pinned with the eight new members; `UpgradeBranch` with Home's five); `EffectOpsTests` (the new ops act while active); `UpgradeTreeTests` (Layout for Home's venue: only Home's, a band per category, a chain in its slot; BranchesOf; Problems allows a Home prerequisite at Home and refuses one at Orders or a branch of the other venue); `ContentSheetMapTests` (the `home` sheets round-trip); `HousePolicyTests` (the reserve, the cheapest buyable, nothing when nothing is buyable, care at the threshold).

## 9. The balance simulation's buying policy

`BalanceSimulation` runs each style twice more with the House policy ("buyer"), after the bills each night: care first (a member at condition 3 or more, while the wallet covers it and stays above the reserve), then the cheapest buyable house upgrade while the wallet stays above `HouseReserve` after the price (one purchase a night). The summary adds, per style and pace: upgrades bought (mean count, the most common first five and their mean day), upkeep paid, break-ins and their losses, care paid, and the wallet's curve and lowest point, next to the runs without the policy (the before/after).

## 10. Open questions for Saleh (the build takes the first option of each; all six are provisional until he answers)

1. **Where the tree's numbers live.** **A (recommended)** the content spreadsheet (`homeUpgrades` sheet, Generate World writes the assets): one sheet to edit 18 rows. **B** hand-authored `UpgradeSO`/`EffectSO` assets in the Inspector (S HM2's way): each upgrade two assets.
2. **The break-in.** **A (recommended)** from day 2, 8 % a night, a quarter of a positive wallet up to 60 cr. **B** rarer (4 %) but bigger (half, up to 150 cr). **C** no break-in: Security only lowers a new "insurance" upkeep.
3. **Mood's effect.** **A (recommended)** mood raises a sick member's nightly chance to recover (4 % a point, up to 50 %). **B** mood is shown only (flavour), no mechanic. **C** mood also trims the slot machine's cost (a content household gambles less).
4. **Upkeep when the wallet is short.** **A (recommended)** billed like rent (the wallet may go negative; bankruptcy decides). **B** a subscription lapses (its effect pauses) on a night the wallet is below 0.
5. **Selling back or downgrading** (the better flat raises rent for good). **A (recommended)** no: a purchase is final, like the office's. **B** a "Move back down" that refunds nothing and removes the flat's rent.
6. **Prices against phase 23's economy** (perfect play nets about 15 cr a day). **A (recommended)** keep §2's prices and let the buyer policy's summary show how many a careful clerk affords; tune after a playtest. **B** raise pay (`basePayPerCorrect` 10 → 12) so a careful clerk buys about half the tree in a run.
