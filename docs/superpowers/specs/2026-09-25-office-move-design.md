# The office move: the game in the art office (design and record)

*2026-09-25 · decisions made by Claude under Saleh's instructions ("cant you just pull the current art scene and use it?", "you are still testing in the 2d scene", "you are using old art assets") and the coordinator's briefs, open to his review · branch `feat/office-move` from `main` at `35fec38`, merged with the local `art` branch at `23aa6e1`, then pieces 4 (characters), 8 (wheel content), the piece 4/5 reconciliation and 6 (the UI reacts to history)*

The game leaves the builder-built 2D booth and runs in the art side's 3D office. The art stays the art side's: the game is a layer on top that finds the art's places by name and never edits the art scene.

## 1. Decisions

| Id | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| M1 | **Two scenes.** `OfficeScene.unity` is the art side's (taken as-is from `art` 23aa6e1) and the active scene (its lighting). All gameplay lives in the additive `OfficeGameplay.unity`, built only by `Build Office UI`, which never opens the art scene. Loading the art office by any path loads the gameplay layer on top (`OfficeScenes`, a `BeforeSceneLoad` hook on `sceneLoaded`); playing the gameplay layer alone loads the art under it. The build list has the gameplay layer right after the art office. | The art keeps its scene; the builder stays authoritative over its own scene; no merge fights over one file. |
| M2 | **A scene contract.** `OfficeContract` (Domain: the anchor ids, `Anchor_{id}` naming, the resolution order and source rules), `OfficeSceneContractSO` (per id: fallback paths or bare names, an optional default pose; the art scene's leftover roots), `OfficeAnchors` (resolution, bounds from active renderers, the PC glass by renderer or material name `Glass`/`Screen`), `OfficeSceneBinder` (`[DefaultExecutionOrder(-1000)]`: places everything at load, logs one line of fallbacks and one warning of defaults/missing). Tools: `Check Office Scene Contract` (report) and `Add Gameplay Anchors (art office)` (for the art side). Documented in `docs/SCENE_CONTRACT_GAMEPLAY.md`. | The art changes without telling the code; a missing place degrades to a working default and says so. |
| M3 | **The art scene's leftovers are neutralised from code, not edited.** The contract's legacy roots (`GameManager`, `DaySystem`, `EventSystem`, `Canvas`, `OfficeOverlayCanvas`, `OfficeRoot`, `Cameras/MonitorVCam`) are switched off at `sceneLoaded` (after their `Awake`, before any `Start`); `GameManager.Start` and `InvestigationUIController.Awake` do nothing in the art scene; the binder disables the camera's `Physics2DRaycaster`. The retired push-in rig stays as an empty `CinemachineCameraRig` stub so `OfficeRoot` loads without a missing-script warning. The art side deletes the leftovers (contract doc). | "Do NOT hand-edit their visual scene." |
| M4 | **3D input only.** A `PhysicsRaycaster` on the art camera (added at runtime, event mask = the `Interactable` layer); every clickable is a `BoxCollider` in the gameplay layer placed over its art's bounds; `Clickable` no longer requires a 2D collider; papers drag with a 3D proxy; a small box inside a bigger one (the power knob inside the PC) is slid along the view ray in front of it. | The art's own colliders never take clicks; the gameplay layer owns every hit area. |
| M5 | **The PC frame (ReStory-like), not the push-in.** Clicking the PC opens a front-facing monitor frame on the overlay canvas, left of centre, the room visible on its right (placeholder bezel art, red close X, power button and LED). The desktop is a World Space canvas on its own `PCDesktop` layer far below the office; the frame camera (ortho, that layer only) draws it into the frame's glass by setting its viewport to the glass's screen rectangle, and is the canvas's event camera. The push-in, `MonitorFraming`, `ICameraRig` and the rig are retired; `BoothRules` loses `Settled` and the focus-exit zone. | Saleh's reference; the desktop stays one canvas with real input. |
| M6 | **The office PC shows the same desktop.** A clone camera renders the desktop into a mipmapped texture, shown on the art's glass submesh by `TimeDesk/PlanarScreen`, a planar projection in the glass's object space (its two widest axes, turned upright for the viewer, letterboxed 4:3 at 92% fill) that swaps the glass's material at runtime; screen off restores the art's material and stops the clone camera. | The art's CRT mesh is not readable and its UVs are not a screen rectangle, so a projection replaces UVs (deviation from the brief's UV suggestion). |
| M7 | **The desk in 3D.** Papers are lit quads lying on the desk plane (the art mat's top), stacked by height (1.5 mm steps, the held one 2 cm up); the drag clamp is the mat's area plus the scanner's drop area; the scanner is a gameplay-owned placeholder flatbed at a free desk spot (default pose) until the art adds one; papers slide from and back to the hand-over point. | The mat is where papers belong; a stand-in keeps the scanner working today. |
| M8 | **The traveller** is piece 4's layered figure as a yaw-only billboard at `Anchor_Traveller` (default (0, 0, 1.6)), 1.8 m tall, tinted (0.9, 0.88, 0.84) so the unlit sprites sit in the room's light; the hit zone runs from the desk top to above the head; the wheel and the bubble centre on the shoulders. | Judged from the screenshots: the NEXT sign hides the legs, as a counter would. |
| M9 | **Readouts are the art's texts.** Day, stability (amber below 60, red below 30), credits (ding) and the digital shift clock are written into `DayNumber`, `StabilityPercent`, `CreditsNumber`, `ShiftClockDisplay`; the NEXT caption is the game's (`desk.readyCaption`); without them a small fallback HUD shows. The analog placeholder clock (`ShiftClock.HandAngles`) is retired. | The art already made them. |
| M10 | **Hover** draws a hull around the hovered art (`TimeDesk/HoverHull`, `Graphics.RenderMesh`, 6 mm), replacing the sprite outline (`OutlineMask`, `SpriteOutlineBuilder` retired). | Works on any art mesh with no per-prop setup. |
| M11 | **The bubble sits above the traveller** (`bubbleOffset` (0, 290)), clear of the wheel's top item; tooltips hang from the top of their prop's art. | In the art office's framing, "beside" put the bubble under the wheel's right item, and a tooltip on the till covered the credits it reads. |
| M12 | **Piece 6 in the move.** The frame's bezel, LED and brand plate take a new diegetic role, `DiegeticDevice` (hardware looks the same in every culture; neutral override fill white, ink `#5E5446`); the builder tags every gameplay-layer graphic; the art's own texts (NEXT) keep the art's font and colour (theming them is deferred). | The theme belongs to the PC's UI, not to the room. |
| M13 | **Build fixes found on the way (art-owned files noted).** `Assets/80s_Office/Scripts/ReplaceGameObjects.cs` wrapped in `#if UNITY_EDITOR` (it broke player builds); `Assets/_Recovery/0 (1).unity` left out of the build list (the art side deletes it); `OfficeScene_HybridArt.unity` deleted (stale). | Player scripts must compile. |
| M14 | **The frame's surround is a `ClickCatcher`, not a Button**, and the hover highlighter never outlines a transparent graphic. | The highlighter's UI outline copies a graphic's quad at full opacity: on the full-screen transparent catcher it painted the whole screen navy (found by the play-through). |
